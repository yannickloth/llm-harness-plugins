# English AI-Prose Pattern Registry

**Scope**: English language prose patterns only. These patterns may appear in AI-generated English text but are not determinative of authorship. Some patterns are appropriate for specific domains (medical, academic, technical, educational, professional).

## Pattern Categories

### Structural Patterns

#### Transition Word Stacking
- **Description**: Excessive use of formal transition words in sequence or close proximity
- **Example**: "However, furthermore, moreover, it is important to note that..."
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Technical documentation where precision transitions aid clarity
- **Suggested revision**: Use one meaningful transition per logical boundary; vary transition types
- **Research sources**: Academic writing studies on cohesion vs. over-connection

#### Formulaic Opening/Closing
- **Description**: Standardized phrases for paragraph beginnings and endings
- **Example**: "In conclusion,", "It is worth noting that,", "This demonstrates that..."
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Academic abstracts, executive summaries where formulaic structure is conventional
- **Suggested revision**: Direct statements without meta-commentary; vary sentence structures
- **Research sources**: Rhetorical analysis of AI-generated academic abstracts

#### Excessive Parallelism
- **Description**: Overuse of balanced, parallel sentence structures beyond natural variation
- **Example**: "Not only X, but also Y. Both A and B. Neither C nor D."
- **Severity**: Suggestion
- **Domain exceptions**: Technical specifications, legal writing where parallelism aids precision
- **Suggested revision**: Break parallel structures; vary sentence complexity naturally
- **Research sources**: Stylometric analysis of sentence structure diversity

#### Paragraph Uniformity
- **Description**: Paragraphs follow identical structural templates (topic sentence → evidence → conclusion)
- **Example**: Every paragraph follows identical 3-sentence pattern
- **Severity**: Strong Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Highly structured technical documentation
- **Suggested revision**: Vary paragraph length, structure, and information density
- **Research sources**: Textual entropy measures in writing analysis

### Lexical Patterns

#### Hedging Stacking
- **Description**: Multiple uncertainty markers in single sentences
- **Example**: "It might be suggested that potentially could indicate..."
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Medical writing where appropriate uncertainty is required
- **Suggested revision**: One hedge per claim maximum; use precise qualifiers
- **Research sources**: Academic hedging patterns analysis

#### Abstract Noun Overuse
- **Description**: Preference for abstract nouns over concrete verbs/actions
- **Example**: "The implementation of the utilization of the facilitation of..."
- **Severity**: Strong Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Theoretical writing where abstraction is conceptually necessary
- **Suggested revision**: Convert nominalizations to active verbs; prefer concrete language
- **Research sources**: Nominalization patterns in academic vs. AI writing

#### Corporate Jargon Preferences
- **Description**: Tendency toward "optimize," "leverage," "streamline," "facilitate"
- **Example**: "To optimize the leveraging of synergistic opportunities..."
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Business/technical documentation where jargon is domain-standard
- **Suggested revision**: Use plain English; choose specific verbs over generic business terms
- **Research sources**: Corporate language pattern analysis

#### Qualifier Accumulation
- **Description**: Stacking adjectives/adverbs without semantic addition
- **Example**: "comprehensive, holistic, integrated, systematic approach"
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Technical specifications where precision requires multiple modifiers
- **Suggested revision**: One precise modifier per concept; remove redundant qualifiers
- **Research sources**: Adjective/adverb density studies

#### Vocabulary Compression
- **Description**: Limited vocabulary diversity within semantic domains
- **Example**: Repeated use of "important," "significant," "crucial" instead of varied vocabulary
- **Severity**: Suggestion
- **Domain exceptions**: Technical writing with controlled terminology
- **Suggested revision**: Use domain-specific varied vocabulary; avoid repetitive emphasis words
- **Research sources**: Lexical diversity metrics (MTLD, VOCD)

### Syntactic Patterns

#### Passive Voice Preference
- **Description**: Higher-than-normal passive voice frequency
- **Example**: "It was observed that..." instead of "We observed that..."
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Scientific writing where passive voice is conventional
- **Suggested revision**: Use active voice unless passive serves specific purpose
- **Research sources**: Passive voice frequency in academic writing

#### Sentence Length Uniformity
- **Description**: Sentences cluster around similar lengths (15-25 words)
- **Example**: Paragraph of 8 sentences all 18-22 words long
- **Severity**: Suggestion
- **Domain exceptions**: Technical documentation where uniformity aids readability
- **Suggested revision**: Vary sentence length naturally (short for impact, long for complexity)
- **Research sources**: Sentence length distribution analysis

#### Complex Sentence Standardization
- **Description**: Preference for specific complex sentence patterns (subordinate clause before main)
- **Example**: "Although X, Y. While A, B. Despite C, D."
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Academic writing where concession structures are conventional
- **Suggested revision**: Vary sentence complexity patterns; use simple sentences for emphasis
- **Research sources**: Syntactic complexity diversity studies

#### Punctuation Predictability
- **Description**: Regular patterns in comma usage, semicolon placement
- **Example**: Every complex sentence uses identical comma pattern
- **Severity**: Suggestion
- **Domain exceptions**: Technical writing with punctuation style guides
- **Suggested revision**: Vary punctuation for natural rhythm; break predictable patterns
- **Research sources**: Punctuation pattern analysis in text generation

### Rhetorical Patterns

#### False Balance Constructions
- **Description**: Artificial "on the other hand" constructions without genuine tension
- **Example**: "While some argue X, others argue Y" when X and Y aren't actually opposed
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Genuine balanced discussions of competing viewpoints
- **Suggested revision**: Present perspectives directly; avoid artificial balance where none exists
- **Research sources**: Rhetorical analysis of AI-generated balance

#### Over-Explanation Tendency
- **Description**: Explaining concepts beyond target audience's likely needs
- **Example**: Defining basic terms in specialist documents
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Educational materials where scaffolding is intentional
- **Suggested revision**: Match explanation depth to audience; assume appropriate background knowledge
- **Research sources**: Audience adaptation studies in technical writing

#### Teaching Tone Markers
- **Description**: Pedagogical framing in non-educational contexts
- **Example**: "Let's explore," "It's helpful to understand," "We can see that"
- **Severity**: Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Educational content, tutorials, where teaching tone is appropriate
- **Suggested revision**: Direct exposition without meta-commentary; vary rhetorical distance
- **Research sources**: Tone analysis in AI-generated educational content

#### Summary Inflation
- **Description**: Summarizing content just presented without adding value
- **Example**: "In summary, what we've discussed above shows that..." repeating previous points
- **Severity**: Strong Recommendation
- **Domain exceptions**: Executive summaries, abstracts where summarization is the purpose
- **Suggested revision**: Move forward to new insights; avoid circular summarization
- **Research sources**: Redundancy analysis in AI-generated text

#### Point-Form Proliferation
- **Description**: Excessive use of bullet points for non-list content
- **Example**: Using bullets for simple sequential statements better as prose
- **Severity**: Suggestion
- **Domain exceptions**: Technical documentation where list format is conventional
- **Suggested revision**: Use prose for narratives; bullets for genuine lists/comparisons
- **Research sources**: List vs. prose effectiveness studies

## Severity Guidelines

### Suggestion
- Minor style improvement
- Doesn't harm comprehension
- Often matters of taste or convention
- Fix if it aligns with desired voice

### Recommendation
- Noticeable AI-style pattern
- May affect reader engagement
- Pattern is clear and present
- Fix for more natural writing

### Strong Recommendation
- Heavy AI-style presence
- Significantly impacts readability
- Pattern appears frequently or prominently
- Fix for professional-quality writing

## Exception Rules

### Technical Documentation
- Formulaic structures acceptable for clarity
- Precision over style in specifications
- Controlled vocabulary expected
- Parallelism aids comprehension

### Academic Writing
- Hedging appropriate for uncertainty
- Passive voice conventional in sciences
- Abstract language may be conceptually necessary
- Citation patterns follow disciplinary conventions

### Medical/Scientific Writing
- Precision modifiers required for accuracy
- Standardized terminology expected
- Cautious language appropriate for uncertainty
- Domain-specific jargon is standard

### Educational Materials
- Teaching tone appropriate and intentional
- Scaffolding explanations expected
- Progressive complexity natural
- Summarization aids learning

## Domain-Specific Notes

### Medical Documentation
- **Acceptable patterns**: Hedging (uncertainty), passive voice (objectivity), abstract nouns (concepts)
- **Problematic patterns**: False balance (evidence clarity), teaching tone (professional context)
- **Context**: Medical writing prioritizes precision and appropriate uncertainty over stylistic variety

### Academic Research
- **Acceptable patterns**: Hedging (claims complexity), passive voice (convention), nominalization (concepts)
- **Problematic patterns**: False balance (genuine controversy required), summary inflation (novel contribution emphasis)
- **Context**: Academic writing has established conventions that may appear "AI-like" but are disciplinary standards

### Technical Documentation
- **Acceptable patterns**: Parallelism (clarity), formulaic structures (reproducibility), uniformity (consistency)
- **Problematic patterns**: Over-explanation (audience mismatch), teaching tone (professional context)
- **Context**: Technical docs prioritize clarity and consistency over stylistic variety

## Pattern Interaction

Patterns often appear in combination. Common clusters:

**AI-Style Cluster 1**: Transition stacking + hedging stacking + summary inflation
**AI-Style Cluster 2**: Excessive parallelism + teaching tone + over-explanation
**AI-Style Cluster 3**: Paragraph uniformity + sentence length uniformity + vocabulary compression

## Limitations

- Patterns based on observed tendencies, not definitive indicators
- Human writers naturally use some of these patterns
- Domain conventions may require "AI-like" structures
- AI writing evolves; patterns may change over time
- Cultural and stylistic differences affect what appears "natural"
- Non-native English writing may share patterns with AI writing
